The problem

LLM-assisted writing produces plausible-looking citations that do not exist, carry the wrong year, name the wrong venue, or point at retracted work. Checking forty references by hand is the bottleneck before submission — and the one step everyone skips.

bibcheck automates it. Point it at your .bib and it tells you which references are wrong, fabricated, or retracted.

Usage

$ bibcheck refs.bibHuman-readable report to stdout.
$ bibcheck refs.bib --tex paper.texAlso cross-check which keys are cited, and which \cite keys have no entry.
$ bibcheck refs.bib --json report.jsonMachine-readable output.
$ bibcheck refs.bib --fix fixed.bibCorrected metadata written to a new file. Keys and field order preserved; anything uncertain left untouched.
$ bibcheck refs.bib --fail-on criticalNonzero exit for CI.
$ bibcheck refs.bib --offlineCache only, no network.

How it behaves

Nothing crashes the run. A timeout, a 500, an unparseable entry — each degrades to a status on one reference. Thirty-nine good references are never lost to one bad one.

Cosmetic differences are not findings. Titles are compared through a normalized key: LaTeX accents, protective braces, punctuation, case and "&" versus "and" all collapse before anything is flagged. Caf\'{e} and Café are the same title.

"We didn't check" is never reported as "it's fine." A DOI that failed to resolve, a lookup that timed out, and a work the registry has never heard of are three different outcomes, and the report keeps them apart.

Every response is cached on disk, including the 404s — so re-runs are instant and --offline genuinely works.
